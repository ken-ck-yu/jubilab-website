The order-to-result layer for lab work

Power lab operations, from sample order to result.

One order format, one status feed, one result schema — across every lab you use, for every scientist and agent who orders.

Jubilab is the order-to-result layer for lab work: one way to place a sequencing order, follow the sample and the run, and get the result back as structured data. Early access is open. We are setting up and building with early adopters, mainly UK biotech startups and university research groups, together with the wet labs that run their samples, starting with non-clinical genetics samples. The order layer is still being built and nothing is released yet.

Today an order moves by Excel and email.

You send samples out and wait. This is what we found when we looked at how sequencing and assay orders move in October 2026.

Two sides, managed separately, joined only by email and Excel. The dry lab side is the scientists and researchers who order, and the people who consume the results. The wet lab side is the lab staff and logistics who receive the sample, run it and send the results back.

  • Order

    A provider-specific Excel manifest, filled in and emailed or re-uploaded, often with a printed copy in the box. Novogene reviews every manifest by hand in one to two hours and approves by email. TU Dresden’s purpose-built portal falls back to emailed Excel above 30 samples.

  • Status

    An email to a rep or project manager. Genohub, the most structured marketplace we looked at, shows six coarse states. None of them is “received”, “QC passed” or “sequencing started”. Separately, in one documented case an order ran 4.5 months against a 10 to 12 week promise, with no delay notice. We do not name the provider.

  • Result

    An emailed link or a BaseSpace invite that expires in 30 days. Provider IDs that the lab maps back to its own sample names by hand. Failed samples that are silently missing.

  • APIs

    We found no ordering API at the sequencing providers we opened (Azenta/GENEWIZ, Novogene, Plasmidsaurus, Eurofins, Psomagen, Edinburgh Genomics, NWGC), or at the research biomarker services (Olink, SomaLogic, Metabolon, IQVIA Labs, Quanterix). The programmatic interfaces we did find sit inside the lab: Clarity LIMS, BaseSpace, MinKNOW, SMRT Link.

  • Under contract

    Pharma and consumer-genomics companies with multi-year MSAs still send manifests out and get scheduled file drops back. Clinical-trial data transfer agreements write “we will SFTP you a file every Friday” into the contract, and data management reconciles by hand.

  • Your ELN

    Benchling and Labguru track samples, not external orders. Procurement issues the PO and stops. Benchling shipped direct ordering in 2025 for Twist, Adaptyv and Ginkgo, and left sequencing and assays out.

Three hand-offs have no structured status, and all three are inside the provider: receipt and QC, the run, and delivery. That is where Jubilab starts.

Three objects, from request to result.

Jubilab is three specifications and the tooling around them. The snippets are drafts of version 0. The field names and values are illustrative and will change before anything ships.

1. Order spec

What the requester sends. Platform, chemistry, depth or output target, a sample manifest, plate layout for assays, the deliverables you want, turnaround and a use tier. One format, whichever lab runs it.

2. Event model

The shared status vocabulary. Every lab reports the same events: order.accepted, kit.shipped, sample.received, qc.passed or qc.failed with a reason, library.prepped, run.started, run.progress, results.delivered and invoice.issued.

3. Result manifest

What the lab emits. A results.json that points at the files with checksums, reports QC for each sample as structured fields, maps the provider’s IDs back to your sample names, and puts a certainty tag on every fact. A failed sample shows up as a failed sample, not as a missing row.

Every fact says how sure we are.

In the draft manifest, each fact carries one of three tags. A gap stays a gap.

  • verified

    Reported by the lab’s own system or instrument, for example by a webhook or run telemetry.

  • inferred

    Read from something less direct, such as an email or a courier update. It stays inferred until the lab confirms it.

  • unknown

    The lab did not say. The manifest keeps the gap and does not fill it in.

Nothing changes on your side on day one.

For labs, cores and boutique providers, and for the teams that use them. You join in the way that suits you, and you can stop at any step. Early access is open, but none of this is released yet.

  • Your existing tools

    We read the order emails and courier tracking you already have, and take a webhook from your LIMS. Facts we read from emails are tagged inferred. Facts that come from your LIMS webhook are tagged verified.

  • A Jubilab UI, API and MCP

    A status page with four buttons (received, QC, run started, results ready) and drag-and-drop result upload that writes a valid result manifest. There is also an API and an MCP for labs that want them. Less work than writing the email.

  • Your instruments

    For labs we partner with or own: run data from MinKNOW or BaseSpace, read live. We read it. We do not control the instrument.

You keep your intake, your LIMS and your instruments. Take orders from software agents and institutional buyers without changing how you work.

Every outside lab, one order format.

For biotech platform teams, startup research groups and pharma sourcing. You send 30 to 150 samples a month across a plasmid service, a WGS provider and a proteomics vendor, order by email and track it in a spreadsheet next to your ELN. This is what we plan to give you. None of it is released yet.

  • Benchling first

    Orders and results land in your ELN. Benchling is the first integration we plan to build. It covers the sequencing and assays that its own ordering does not.

  • One agreement

    One MSA with us in place of a different NDA and onboarding for every vendor. The terms are not set yet.

  • A history of every order

    Each status change is an append-only event with a time and a source. You can see what changed and when.

  • Agent and MCP access

    An MCP server and a REST API so your agents can quote, order, check status and fetch results, with the same rules as a person.

You have negotiated the price. We aim to make what flows under the contract machine-readable, so adding a second lab does not need a bespoke integration. Whether the lab is across the hall or across the world, the plan is the same order object. Benchling, Labguru and Scispot are names of their owners. Jubilab is not affiliated with them.

Why now: every AI agent stops at the lab door.

Agents can already draft a sequencing plan and reason about a result file. They stop at the lab, because the lab side is not callable: in the providers we opened, we found no ordering API. We are building the door.

At Biligene, our own sequencing orders lived in forty email threads. That is why we are building this, and why Biligene’s own sequencing orders are planned to run through it first.

For developers and agent builders.

The plan is an MCP server and a REST API with five calls, plus webhooks for events. None of it is released, so there is nothing to install yet and no endpoint to call. We would rather shape the surface with the people who will use it.

What we don’t do.

  • No instrument control

    Jubilab reads what a lab reports. It does not drive sequencers, liquid handlers or any other instrument.

  • No marketplace

    We do not broker quotes or price, and we are not a place to shop for labs. You keep your providers and your contracts. Jubilab standardizes the order, the status and the result.

  • No clinical diagnostics

    Human clinical testing is out of scope. It needs a physician of record, CLIA certification and critical-value duties, and its own standards already exist.

Questions

  • What is Jubilab?

    Jubilab is the order-to-result layer for lab work: a standard way to place a lab order (sequencing first, biomarker assays next), track the sample and the run, and get the result back in a machine-readable shape, for scientists and for the AI agents that work for them. Early access is open, but the order layer is still being built and nothing on this page is released yet.

  • Can I use it today?

    Not yet. Early access is open: we are setting up and building with early adopters, and you can ask to be one of them. The order layer is still being built and nothing is released yet. The JSON examples on this page are drafts of the planned formats and will change.

  • Who is it for?

    We are recruiting early adopters, mainly UK biotech startups and university research groups (groups like those at UCL or Cambridge are the kind we mean, as examples only, not as customers or partners), together with the wet labs that run their samples. We start with non-clinical genetics samples. Biotech teams that send 30 to 150 samples a month to several outside labs, pharma and large organizations with a constant outsourced flow, and the labs and core facilities that take those orders are also welcome to ask.

  • Does a lab have to change how it works?

    No. There are three ways to join, and you can stop at any step. First, your existing tools: we read the order emails and courier tracking you already have, and take a webhook from your LIMS. Second, a Jubilab UI, API and MCP: a status page with four buttons and drag-and-drop result upload, plus an API and MCP if you want them. Third, for labs we partner with or own, your instruments. We work with your existing toolset without disruption and give AI agents a workflow they can call. None of it is released yet.

  • How is it different from an ELN, a LIMS or a marketplace?

    An ELN such as Benchling or Labguru tracks your samples. A LIMS runs inside one lab. A marketplace brokers quotes, contracts and billing between people. Jubilab standardizes the order, the status and the result across labs, and is meant to sit beside the tools you already use. It does not replace them, control instruments or broker price. Product names belong to their owners, and Jubilab is not affiliated with them.

  • What will Jubilab not do?

    It will not control instruments, it will not be a marketplace, and it will not handle human clinical diagnostics. Research sequencing comes first, then research biomarker assays such as proteomics, multiplex immunoassay and metabolomics.

It asks for your role, your organization, the providers you use, how many samples you send out in a month and your ELN, so we can choose the teams and labs we learn from. We are setting up early adopters now, in small groups, and write to you by email.
